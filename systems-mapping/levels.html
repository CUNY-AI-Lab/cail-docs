The Four Levels

Follow the focal person's direct encounters, connections between settings, outside decisions, and broader conditions across one cumulative map.

The Progression

A hospital can enter a map in different ways. Rosa participates in appointments at the hospital, so that immediate setting belongs at Micro. If a billing office makes decisions that reach her without her participation, that outside decision setting belongs at Exo. The focal person's participation guides placement.

The four rings follow Bronfenbrenner's ecological model as the app applies it to mapping. Micro records direct participation and immediate-setting membership. Meso names connections between those settings, Exo traces outside decisions, and Macro identifies broader conditions. Relationship fields record time and process within those four rings.

Part 1, Micro and Meso

Micro

At Micro, map the people and immediate settings the focal person encounters. A role, activity, or object can also belong here when the memo places it in that person's direct experience. Give each direct relationship within a setting a verb, as in Nurse Jo refers Rosa or Librarian Jo runs the workshop. An item that is not itself a setting can name the settings where it appears.

Meso

At Meso, name a linkage between at least two settings the focal person belongs to. In the East Harlem case, Care coordination through paperwork between Metropolitan Hospital and the neighborhood center names what connects Rosa's settings. Add that linkage in the Meso ring and scope it to the existing Micro settings. The dotted scope lines identify the settings it joins. Give the Meso item a name that describes the linkage.

Part 2, Exo and Macro

Exo

At Exo, map an outside setting whose decisions affect the focal person without their participation. A hospital's billing office or a parent's employer can play this role when the case shows an indirect effect. Trace the pathway from the decision setting into one or more mapped Micro settings, and explain how the effect reaches the focal person.

Macro

At Macro, identify a broader condition that shapes the case, such as a law, policy, economic condition, history, or belief. Scope the item to at least two earlier elements and explain each connection through evidence. Place a named organization according to its role in the person's ecology, and use Macro for the broader condition under study.

How Work Carries Forward

Items retain their IDs across levels, so a Meso item refers to the same Micro settings you mapped earlier. A scope link records inclusion or influence without creating a directional relationship. Micro and Exo relationships can also describe frequency, duration, direction, conditions, and change over time.

When you complete a level, the app starts the next level's memo review as part of the transition. After Meso, it opens Review Micro and Meso so you can inspect Part 1 together before beginning Exo. Look for missing settings or unsupported relationships while their evidence remains in view.

Common Placement Questions

For a young person who attends school, the classroom belongs at Micro because they participate there. A district office that sets an attendance rule can belong at Exo when its decisions reach the young person indirectly. Apply the same test to a parent's workplace. If a shift change affects a child who does not participate at that workplace, map the decision setting at Exo.

Housing insecurity names a broader condition for Macro. A housing court or landlord may enter at Exo when the case traces an outside decision to the focal person's setting. For Meso, check that the item joins at least two immediate settings. The app requires both references because the linkage is the object you are mapping.
