Teaching Tips

Use each level to ask students where a claim belongs and which passage supports it.

Before the Semester

Write the four Field Notes questions before you prepare model guidance. Students must answer a question to complete each level, so the questions can connect one week's work with the next. A Micro question can ask students to identify a missing direct relationship and propose a connection between settings to examine at Meso.

Prepare a shared case of about a page for the first two weeks. The app's fictional East Harlem care-coordination case and library-and-garden example offer starting points you can adapt. When everyone reads the same memo, students can trace disagreements about placement to the same source before bringing in their own fieldwork.

Paste the course's definitions into Guidance and materials for Part 1 and Part 2. The model will then receive the terms you teach alongside the app's fixed level rules. Inspect a first set of suggestions before assigning the case so you can identify a placement question worth discussing.

In Class

Project a map and ask students to explain one disputed placement. A hospital setting and its billing office can open a useful distinction between direct participation at Micro and an outside decision at Exo. Ask for the focal person's role in each setting before deciding where the item belongs.

Review a suggestion card together and read its source passage before choosing Add to map, Set aside, or Remove. Students should explain what the passage supports and what they have inferred. Then switch to List to inspect the map for relationships that still lack a verb or an evidence record.

With the Model

Treat the first model pass as a reading to examine. It requests at most six items from the memo, and it has no direct knowledge of the fieldwork beyond the supplied text. Ask students to compare one suggestion with their own reading and explain why they accepted, deferred, or rejected it.

At the end of a level, students can use Map Check on a connection they find uncertain. The model returns at most four notes. Students should compare those notes with the evidence before revising, then preserve one unsupported suggestion or mistaken placement for class discussion. The error gives the class a specific claim to test.

Assessment

Assess how students use the evidence fields to support and qualify a claim. The source passage should allow you to retrace their reasoning, while uncertainty and counterevidence should show where they would revise it. A missing-perspectives note can identify whose account they need next.

Ask for a submitted revision after each level so you can read how the map changes across the course. Use Field Notes to plan feedback around one gap and one next observation. When a student revises the map, compare the new evidence with the question they set themselves at the previous level.
