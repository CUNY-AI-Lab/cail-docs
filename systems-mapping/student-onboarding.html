Student Onboarding

Use one shared case during the first week so you can help students reach a saved Micro map.

Before Day One

Send the course invitation from the Lab portal, then publish your Field Notes questions and Part 1 guidance in the Instructor Console. Post the case memo with a link to the app and these documentation pages on your course site. Students will then have the same source and entry point when class begins.

Day One (20 minutes)

Sign in together with CUNY Login and ask students to choose the course under Your courses. Each student should create a project named after the case and paste the same memo. Pause at the transfer disclosure and read it aloud before anyone requests a model review.

Choose Review the memo and discuss the first three suggestions as a class. Open Details to compare each suggestion with its passage, then explain the choice to add, set aside, or remove it. Before students leave, ask each person to make one decision and open its Details. You can use that action to check that everyone has reached the review task.

First Week

Assign Memo to Map as homework on the shared case, through Finish review and the Micro Field Notes response. On day three or four, use Sort the Suggestions to compare the model's reading with the memo. Remind students that the model receives the supplied text and has not visited the field site.

Ask for a first submitted revision by the end of the week. Open Students and saved progress to check the roster and follow up with anyone whose first map remains pending. A missing map may signal an access problem or work saved in the general workspace.

Explaining Privacy

Tell students when you can see their work. You can see that a course map exists and which level and stage it has reached. You read its content when they submit a revision, or when they share a project link that you can open as a course member. Your instructor role gives you no access to their general-workspace maps.

Common First-Week Problems

If a student cannot see the course, check whether they accepted the invitation and signed in with the CUNY identity tied to it. Compare that identity with the roster before asking them to start again.

If a review stops, read the app's explanation. A timed-out request or invalid response stops without a silent retry, and the student can choose Review the memo again. Reloading recovers a result that already finished for the same request without starting another one.

If a student cannot continue to Meso, check for an empty Micro Field Notes response or an undecided suggestion. If they cannot find a map, check the course and general workspaces on Projects. Maps remain in the workspace where students created them, and the app preserves a divergent browser copy for recovery export.

For another problem, use Report a problem. The report identifies the verified account and excludes map content, so students should describe what they were doing and what happened.
