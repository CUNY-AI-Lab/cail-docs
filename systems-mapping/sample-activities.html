Sample Activities

Give students a short mapping task that asks them to inspect a source, make a choice, and explain it.

These activities can fit a class period or a short homework assignment. Each describes what students should learn, what you need to prepare, and what to inspect in the resulting map. Model review is optional unless the activity specifically examines model output.

1. Memo to Map

Use a shared one-page case to help students identify direct relationships around a focal person. Begin at Micro with Automatic suggestion addition off so students decide on each model suggestion themselves.

Create a project and write an inquiry that names the focal person and one question about them. Paste the memo, then write down the people and places you expect to map before requesting a review. Compare the model's suggestions with your list and the source. Use Add item for a supported item the model missed, and give every relationship a verb and a source passage.

When a student places the hospital's billing department at Micro, ask whether the focal person participates there. If the case describes an outside decision that reaches the person indirectly, save that decision setting for Exo. The student's explanation should establish the placement.

2. Sort the Suggestions

Use one model review to examine whether a suggestion follows from a passage. Any memo will work, although a shared text lets students compare decisions against the same evidence.

Review the memo and open Details on each suggestion before deciding. Record whether it cites a passage and whether that passage supports the label. Add supported items, set aside plausible inferences that need evidence, and remove suggestions the memo contradicts. Choose one removed suggestion and explain its claim, the source's account, and your hypothesis about why the model produced it.

Pair students who reviewed the same memo and ask them to compare their choices. When they disagree, return to the passage and identify whether the dispute concerns the model's wording, the level, or the inference itself.

3. Name the Mesosystem

Use a completed Micro map with at least three settings to distinguish a link between settings from a direct relationship within one. Students begin this exercise at Meso.

Compare pairs of Micro settings and look for something that passes between them, such as information, paperwork, or expectations. For a supported pair, create a Meso item that names the linkage and scope it to both settings. Cite the passage in its evidence record, or mark the linkage as an inference if the memo leaves it implicit. Then answer the Meso Field Notes question.

A student may write “the clinic calls the school” as the item label. Ask what the call carries and how it connects the settings. A label such as “appointment reminders between the clinic and the school” identifies the linkage for the Meso ring.

4. Follow the Decision

After Part 1, ask students to trace an indirect effect from an outside decision to the focal person. Add a short reading about a relevant institution to the course materials and begin at Exo.

Choose an event in the memo that affects the focal person without their participation, such as a schedule change or a closed program. Name the setting where someone made that decision and add it at Exo. Draw the pathway to the Micro setting it affects, then describe how often the effect occurs, which conditions govern it, and how it changes over time.

Use Check this connection if you want the model to examine the pathway. Compare its notes with your evidence and record where you agree or disagree. A useful revision makes the indirect effect easier for another reader to trace.

5. Scope the Condition

Use this exercise at Macro after students complete Exo. Provide a policy summary so students can connect a broader condition to specific elements already on their maps.

Name one law, policy, economic condition, or belief that shapes the case. Add it at Macro and scope it to at least two earlier items. Explain each scope in the evidence record and add counterevidence that shows where the condition may not apply. Export a PNG to bring to class.

Inspect the label before discussing the scopes. State attendance law names a Macro condition. The Department of Education names an institution whose placement depends on its role in the case, such as an Exo decision setting. Ask students to identify the rule and the office separately before they revise.

6. Field Notes Exchange

Pair students in the same course and have them share project links. The exercise asks each reader to retrace a classmate's reasoning through the evidence on the map.

Open your partner's link in List view. Find a relationship that needs a source passage and an item whose level you would question. Write a short note that explains both concerns and identifies something their map helped you notice. Use your partner's response to revise your own map, then submit the revision.

Adapting These Activities

In an asynchronous course, use submitted revisions and recorded feedback to replace an in-class comparison. In a large section, begin with Memo to Map and Sort the Suggestions on the shared case, then assign the next three exercises to students' fieldwork memos.

Each memo review or Map Check starts a request with a 90-second budget. See Privacy & Limits for the request limits. Reading and editing an existing map makes no model request. You can keep the work focused by asking students to explain one decision before requesting another pass.
