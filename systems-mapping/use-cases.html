Use Cases

Adapt a shared case, a community inquiry, or field-placement notes into a sequence of mapping assignments.

These plans connect short exercises with work that develops across several weeks. The first uses the app's fictional East Harlem care-coordination case. The others were designed for Understanding Systems Impacting Youth and Families, a Fall 2026 course at Hunter College. Adapt the readings and schedule to the people and settings your course examines.

Use Case 1. Care Coordination in East Harlem

Use this two-week Part 1 unit when students need a common case for learning the levels. Rosa receives care at Metropolitan Hospital, where Nurse Jo refers her to a neighborhood center. Denise and Marisol are family members, and paperwork connects the hospital with the center. Every student pastes the same one-page memo so the class can discuss differences in interpretation.

For Micro, use the default Field Notes question about what students noticed and which items could help them begin Meso. Write a Meso question that asks which linkage matters most to Rosa. Plan one model review at each level if you want students to examine suggestions.

During week one, students review the memo and give each direct relationship a verb, such as Nurse Jo refers Rosa or Marisol accompanies Rosa. Their Field Notes identify a missing relationship. During week two, they add Care coordination through paperwork at Meso, scope it to the hospital and neighborhood center, and cite the passage that supports it. Open Review Micro and Meso together to compare the two levels.

Ask students to explain whether the case places Denise in Rosa's household setting or elsewhere. If the model suggests a billing office at Micro, return to Rosa's participation in that office. An outside decision that affects her indirectly belongs at Exo and can wait for Part 2. The map should make those placement decisions traceable to the case.

Give students a prompt that begins with Rosa's immediate world. Ask them to map whom she sees and where she goes, support every direct arrow with a verb and a sentence from the memo, then name something that passes between two of her settings. Their Meso item should identify that linkage.

Use Case 2. A Youth's Systems Across the Semester

Use a fourteen-week sequence when the map serves as the course's central assignment. Students build one young person's ecology from Micro through Macro. Part 1 uses a shared case, while Part 2 draws on students' own fieldwork notes, interviews, or reading responses.

In Guidance and materials, publish the course's definitions of setting and participation for Part 1. For Part 2, add a short summary of two institutions and one policy from the syllabus. Ask students to submit a revision after each level so you can follow how their evidence changes.

During weeks one through three, assign Memo to Map followed by Sort the Suggestions, then collect a Micro revision. During weeks four through six, use Name the Mesosystem before students review and submit Part 1. Each stage should leave a question for the next reading.

During weeks seven through ten, students bring a new memo to Exo and complete Follow the Decision. A school district, a parent's employer, or a child-welfare agency may provide the outside decision setting when the memo supports that role. During weeks eleven through thirteen, Scope the Condition connects earlier items to a condition such as housing policy or a school funding formula. Students submit a revision at each level.

In week fourteen, students export PNG and Markdown copies and use their four Field Notes responses to explain the map's development. At the start of Part 2, check the roster for students who have not begun Exo. The shared case may provide little evidence about outside decisions, so help students identify the new material they need before asking them to add items.

Explain the semester assignment as one continuing map. Earlier items remain available as later rings open, and each Field Notes response should identify what the next level needs. Students submit when they finish a level so your feedback can address a specific version.

Use Case 3. Community Asset Map

Begin with what a focal resident can reach in a neighborhood. Students might document a library workshop, a garden, a church kitchen, or a neighbor's repair skill through walking notes or a short interview with a resident or organizer. The app's library-and-garden example, which includes Maya, Librarian Jo, Coordinator Lee, and a shared calendar, offers a starting case.

Set an inquiry that asks what capacities the block already has and who can reach them. In Part 1 guidance, ask the model to attend to capacities and gathering places. Students should use the focal resident's experience to decide which assets belong on the map.

During week one, students map the assets the resident uses directly at Micro. During week two, they name the links between settings at Meso, such as a shared calendar that connects library and garden activities. If a volunteer works in both places, students should explain how that person's work links the settings.

During week three, students ask who decides whether the garden retains its lot, then map the outside decision setting at Exo. They continue to Macro to examine the zoning or funding condition that shapes the decision. The resulting map can support a discussion with a community partner after students review and export a PDF.

Watch for a map that lists every asset on the block without showing the resident's access. Return students to the inquiry and ask which passage or observation establishes that access. Their final map should let a partner trace a resource from direct use through the decisions and conditions that sustain it.

Use Case 4. From Field Placement to Map

Use weekly process notes when students already write about a placement. Each note can supply evidence for the current level, so the map develops alongside their visits. Ask students to de-identify the note before pasting it and to follow the placement's confidentiality rules. The app stores memo text with the project.

Write Field Notes questions that ask for one observation at the next visit, and collect a submitted revision each week. In weeks one and two, students build Micro from an intake note. In weeks three and four, a note about a school conference or a home visit with a clinic referral can support a Meso linkage between settings.

During weeks five through eight, students examine an agency decision the client did not participate in and trace its effect at Exo. During weeks nine through twelve, they pair the placement notes with readings on benefit rules, housing law, or immigration policy for Macro. Read each submitted snapshot and use the student's Field Notes to prepare a supervision question.

Before the first upload, ask students to inspect what their notes reveal even after names change. Problem reports exclude memo text, but a model review sends the supplied memo and exported copies can travel when students share them. Check the intended audience before sharing any copy.

Ask students to map what the week's note supports and use Field Notes to state what they will look for next. At the following visit, they can compare that plan with what they observed and revise the evidence record before submitting.

Use Case 5. Policy Pathway Analysis

Use a completed Part 1 map to investigate how one policy reaches a particular young person. Publish a short policy summary in Part 2 course materials. Students can begin their reading with the policy, while the app's sequence takes them through Exo before Macro.

During week one, students identify the offices or institutions that act on the policy and trace their decisions into mapped Micro settings. They might examine an agency office, a school district, or a landlord, depending on the case. After completing Exo, they add the policy at Macro during week two and scope it to at least two earlier items. Each scope needs an explanation tied to evidence.

During week three, students use Check this connection on the decision pathways they want to examine. Ask them to record where the model's notes agree with the evidence and where the notes overreach. They can then revise and export Markdown as the basis of a case-grounded policy brief.

If a student represents the policy's effect as one direct arrow to the person, ask which office interprets or applies it and which setting receives the effect. The Exo pathway should show that process. The Macro scope then identifies where the broader condition applies.

Choosing a Sequence

Choose a shared case when students need to compare interpretations against the same passage. Choose fieldwork when the inquiry needs evidence the case cannot supply. In either sequence, ask students to preserve uncertainty in their evidence records and use Field Notes to decide what to investigate next.
